Product case study · EdTech · Careers

Closing the Silence Gap.

Why the internship funnel is breaking, and why the fix is showing students the wait, not shortening the search.

As part of a recruitment process for an EdTech platform, I was asked to put together a product case study. This is it.

AI Product Manager · take-home Subject: Virtual Internships August 2026 Public sources only

By Grant Jerry · jerrygrantmax@gmail.com

The case in one paragraph

The worst part of applying isn't rejection. It's silence.

Students are applying more and hearing back less. In two years, applications per internship posting went from 43 to 109 while postings fell 15%, and each recruiter now handles 412% more applications with 56% fewer colleagues. Every big platform is answering this the same way: better search, better ranking, a faster route to the apply button. But that isn't what students say hurts. The worst part isn't rejection, it's silence. 60% say the single most frustrating thing is not knowing whether a human ever looked at their work. So the real opportunity sits behind the apply button, not in front of it.

Virtual Internships is the one platform that already takes the competition problem off the table, because it guarantees a placement. But it describes how long that takes four different ways across its own site, and it never shows a student where they are inside that window. My proposal is simple: make the guarantee something you can actually see.

The argument, in five beats

1

The funnel is jammed, not competitive.

Demand per posting has more than doubled, supply shrank, and the people left to reply got fewer. That's arithmetic, not a ranking problem.

2

The pain that comes out of it is silence.

Ghosting, ghost jobs, no feedback, no status. Students rank this above rejection itself.

3

Everyone is fixing the wrong end.

LinkedIn ranks better, Forage pre-educates, Internshala adds supply. Every one of them still makes the student beat other students.

4

Virtual Internships is built differently.

It guarantees a placement in the student's chosen field, paid for by universities and governments, with host companies paying nothing. The competition problem just isn't there.

5

But it tells the promise, it doesn't show it.

Four different public claims about the same window, and nothing that shows a student their progress. Instrumenting a promise you've already made is about the cheapest product work there is.

Competitive audit

Rank the marketplace

LinkedIn

1.3B members. In Jan 2026 it rolled semantic AI job search out to every English-language member, and its free "Job Match" scores your profile against a posting and tells you where you fall short. LinkedIn says this pushes ~2M applications a month toward better-fitting roles, so it's actively trying to cut volume.

Gap
The "Top Applicant" badge is locked behind Premium, can fire on a pool as small as ten applicants, and only the student ever sees it. The recruiter doesn't. On top of that, an estimated 27.4% of US listings may be stale or ghost postings.
Opportunity
Make the fit signal go both ways, and tell students how fresh a posting is and when to expect a reply.
Pre-educate the applicant

Forage

Free, employer-built job simulations. 9M+ students, 250+ simulations, 90+ employers, acquired by EAB in Apr 2024. There's no matching. You self-select, do the tasks against model answers, and get a certificate.

Gap
Forage's own help centre says partners don't review submissions and the work "does not affect an application positively or negatively," with no individual feedback. And it claims its impact four different ways (3.3x, 2x, 3x, 71%) without publishing the method behind any of them.
Opportunity
Close the loop on the strongest signal in the whole funnel: a student who just spent four unpaid hours on a firm's own tasks. Send that to the employer as a real credential, or say plainly that it's self-study.
Add liquidity and a floor

Internshala

India's largest internship marketplace. 34M users, ~3M active applicants a year, ~450K employers, and upGrad bought ~90% in Feb 2026. A minimum-stipend policy keeps unpaid listings out. It also has the clearest status ladder in the category: Applied → Seen → In-Touch → Under Review → Hired.

Gap
Trust. The loudest complaints are about placement-guarantee courses whose refund depends on five conditions all being met at once. And it says 400K+ employers on one page and 100K+ on another, the same day.
Opportunity
Put a time commitment on each status, expire listings that stall, and rank employers by how they actually respond instead of how much they spend.
Remove the competition — the subject

Virtual Internships

Founded 2018, $18.35M raised, ~9,000 students placed as of Oct 2024. Universities and governments pay per student, host companies pay nothing, and placements are unpaid, remote, project-based, and at least 120 hours. Students also get two 1:1 coaching sessions and the CareerBridge curriculum.

Gap
It states the promise four ways: "roughly six weeks" to students, "less than six weeks" to universities, "45 days or less" to governments, "within one month" in 2022 press. The guarantee covers the career field but explicitly not location. And there's no student-facing progress tracker anywhere in the public record.
Opportunity
The promise is already the product spec. Instrument the six weeks, show the student where they are inside it, and let university partners watch the same clock.

The proposal

Three releases, in order. The cheapest and highest-impact one is just instrumenting a promise Virtual Internships has already made.

P0 · Ship first

Placement Progress Tracker

Six states, visible from the moment you apply. Each one shows the date it started and a real commitment for the next, like "We'll introduce you to a host company by 12 September," not a vague estimate. If a step is about to slip, it changes before the deadline passes and tells the student, instead of waiting to be asked.

Application received→ Profile in matching→ Introduced to host→ Interview scheduled→ Offer made→ Onboarded
How it's built
Every state change is already an event some internal system causes, so nobody's doing new manual work. One computed definition of time-to-placement replaces the four marketing numbers, and the same event stream powers a cohort view for university and government partners.
Left out on purpose
How many other candidates are in the running. Showing that would drag back the exact competition anxiety this model exists to remove.
P1 · The hard one

Match Reasons

A proposed host company shows up with three to five plain-English reasons, pulled from the real matching inputs: career field, project type, weekly hours, time-zone overlap, and the skills named in the project plan.

Say who decided
If a coach made the call, say so. "Your coach picked this because…" beats a made-up algorithm score, and it has the advantage of being true.
Name the compromise
If a preference couldn't be met (location is only guaranteed by field), say it here rather than letting the student find out later. Rejections are structured, not free text, so they double as a supply-gap report.
P2 · Move the check earlier

Eligibility & Readiness Checklist

Work authorisation and location, realistic weekly hours, time-zone tolerance, equipment and connectivity, all asked before matching starts, not after a host company has already interviewed.

Reuse what exists
Readiness maps onto the eight NACE competencies the current curriculum already teaches.
Make it evidence
A finished checklist is a fact the host company can see. It turns "I'm really keen" into something they can verify.
Out of scope

Paid placements; pricing and refunds (business-model calls I have no unit economics for); an open browse-and-apply marketplace (that just rebuilds the volume problem); a mobile app; post-placement mentor matching.

Counter-metric

Time-to-placement itself. A tracker that makes waiting visible can't become a reason to rush someone into a bad match just to keep the clock green.

Guardrail

Host-company satisfaction and repeat-posting rate can't drop. Supply is free here, which makes it easy to take for granted and expensive to lose.

Method & limitations

I built this entirely from public sources: company product pages, help centres and terms of service, published funding and press coverage, and third-party market and student surveys. No user interviews, and no internal product data. Every student-side claim comes from a published survey with a stated sample, from n=1,002 at the smallest to n=20,482 at the largest, across seven surveys.

The surveys tell me that 60% of applicants rank silence as their worst experience. They don't tell me why it lands so hard. So I've written the three proposals as hypotheses I can test, not as conclusions:

H1 — students drop out because the wait feels endless, not because the match is bad.

Testable inside a single cohort by running the tracker on half of it.

H2 — students say no to ambiguity, not to a bad fit.

Needs an A/B on the match-proposal screen.

H3 — a real share of late drop-offs are eligibility problems you could have caught on day one.

Needs withdrawal reason codes, which don't seem to exist yet. Shipping those is a small first release on its own.

Where the public record genuinely runs out: there's no benchmark for internship-specific time-to-first-response, no response-rate data for Internshala, and no study on how often students find eligibility unclear. I didn't fill any of those with guesses.
