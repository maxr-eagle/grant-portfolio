Case study · African fintech 0→1 · Making money trustworthy

Turning scattered finances into one honest view.

Arzìqi is a wealth app for Nigerian and African owner-operators. Mensaah started it. I came in at the concept stage and owned the product from research through to launch.

Role · Product Manager (owner) Stage · Pre-launch Scope · Research → Design → Delivery

What it is

One honest view of what someone owns, owes, and controls, across banks, FX, crypto, brokerage, property, and their businesses.

My role

I owned it 0→1. Joined before there was a frontend and did the research, the brand, the design system, the roadmap, and the build. Helped hire the engineers too.

The big calls

Make trust visible. Keep personal and business separate. Build the design system from scratch. Never fake the data.

Status

Pre-launch, ready for a closed beta. V1 shipped, V2 mostly there. 100+ merge requests across the stack.

The problem

"An owner-operator's wealth is scattered and untrustworthy. There's no single view."

Their money is everywhere: personal and business accounts, domiciliary and FX balances, crypto, property, stakes in private companies, PDFs, email. Nothing pulls it together, because there's no aggregation layer that reaches Nigerian banks. So people fall back on spreadsheets, screenshots, half a dozen banking apps, and their accountant, and still can't really say what they're worth.

Mensaah and I checked this with a mix of surveys and one-on-one interviews about how people track their net worth today. The answers were all over the place, and that was the point. Everyone does it differently and holds a different mix of stuff. That's why Arzìqi had to pull from lots of sources and be upfront about where each number came from, instead of being one tidy tracker.

Who it's for, and a focus call

I went after the owner-operator because their money is the most tangled, which is exactly where pulling it together helps most. Plenty of individual users were keen too, but I kept them as a secondary audience and stayed away from turning Arzìqi into a budgeting app. Serving the hardest user well was the bet.

Where I came in

What I got handed was a concept (it was called Azana back then) and a half-finished product doc. There was a backend, but no frontend at all, so the whole thing people would actually see was still to be figured out and built. My first couple of weeks were research, then a brand, then design. I also ran the hiring for the first three engineers. After that I owned it end to end up to where it is now, roughly in this order: research, brand, design system, build, and I kept trust at the centre of it.

Four calls I made

Each one traces back to the same problem (scattered and untrustworthy, no single view) and to real work that shipped.

01
Framing

Make trust something you can see

The situation
Net worth gets stitched together from sources you can trust to very different degrees: live bank sync, parsed emails, a rough guess, an uploaded statement. One big number hides all of that.
What I did
Put the source, the evidence, and the freshness on every value, right where you can see it. If something's stale, show a way to fix it.
Why
The real problem was trust, not just having things in one place. Adding them up without showing your working just gives you a tidier number you still can't rely on.
How it landed
The app is honest about its own data. Manual entry became a real, respectable way to value something instead of a failure state.
02
Focus

Keep personal and business apart

The situation
An owner-operator's whole thing is that personal and business money blur together. Lump them and the number lies; ignore the business and it's incomplete.
What I did
Separate entities by design. A default Personal one, business ones you attach, ownership shown as a holding inside Personal, and no blended totals that quietly double-count.
Why
To serve the hardest user properly, and to not drift into budgeting or full accounting, which were both off the table.
How it landed
Per-entity dashboards and an all-entities view that doesn't double-count. I also cut the old enterprise demo that didn't belong yet.
03
Design

Build the whole design language

The situation
I was the designer as well as the PM, and there was no frontend. "Calm, premium, trustworthy" had to become actual pixels.
What I did
Built a design system from scratch: tokens, then components, then the shell, then the pages. Small things that signal trust, like a source chip and a quiet sparkline instead of a loud gradient.
Why
In fintech, trust is partly a feeling. A loud or inconsistent UI undercuts it. A system keeps everything consistent, and consistency reads as trustworthy.
How it landed
One coherent look across the app, light and dark. It feels as careful as the data underneath.
04
Delivery

Don't fake the data

The situation
The headline net-worth chart was a fake line. A later "fix" just drew today's numbers backwards, so it came out flat. Still fake.
What I did
Refused to ship made-up history. I pushed for a real backend endpoint that builds the trend from actual data.
Why
The whole problem is trust. A faked chart on the main screen is exactly the thing the product is meant to fix.
How it landed
A real trend you can filter by period, with an honest empty state when there's nothing yet.

Trust you can see

The build

Where it's at

Arzìqi is pre-launch and ready for a closed beta. V1 (ingestion, parsing, transactions, dashboards, anomalies, assets and liabilities) is shipped. V2 (the net-worth dashboard, source-backed records, market pricing, evidence docs, the entity model, billing) is mostly there. I've pushed 100+ merge requests across the rebrand, the design system, entities, onboarding, parser reliability, security, and the dashboard.

It hasn't launched, so there's no traction to point at yet. What's here is the thinking, the craft, and a product that's ready to go.

What I'd do differently

If I did it again, I'd drop the email-first start. Pushing every new user to connect a mailbox first assumed that's how they'd want to begin. It isn't. I later moved onboarding to asset-first, so people can add any account first. The reason it stuck as a lesson is that I didn't just notice it, I shipped the fix.
