Case study · Fintech 0→1

Arzìqi — one trusted view of an owner-operator's wealth.

A wealth command center for Nigerian & African owner-operators. Founded by Mensaah; I was brought in at concept to own the product 0→1 — research, design, and delivery.

Role · Product Manager (owner) Stage · Pre-launch Scope · Research → Design → Delivery

What it is

One trusted view of what an owner-operator owns, owes, and controls — across banks, FX, crypto, brokerage, real estate, and business entities.

My role

0→1 owner. Joined before any frontend existed; drove research, brand, the design system, roadmap, and delivery — and helped recruit the founding engineers.

Key decisions

Make trust first-class; separate personal & business; build the design system from scratch; ship real data, not fabricated charts.

Status

Pre-launch, closed-beta-ready. V1 shipped, V2 partially shipped. 100+ merge requests authored across the stack.

The problem

"An owner-operator's wealth is scattered and untrustworthy — no single view."

Their money lives across personal and business accounts, domiciliary/FX balances, crypto, real estate, private company stakes, PDFs, and email — and there's no aggregation layer that reaches African institutions. So they fall back on spreadsheets, screenshots, bank apps, and accountants, and end up with a weak, stale, low-trust picture of their net worth, with personal and business finances blurred together.

The founder and I validated this with surveys across a few groups and 1:1 interviews on how people actually track their net worth. The answers varied wildly — and that variance was the insight: owner-operators track wealth completely differently and hold completely different asset mixes. That's exactly why Arzìqi had to be multi-source and evidence-led, not a single-method tracker.

Who it's for — a focus decision

I focused on the owner-operator because their finances are the most fragmented — which is precisely where an aggregation product creates the most value. Individual users showed strong interest too, but I kept them as a secondary, default experience and deliberately did not reposition Arzìqi as a consumer budgeting app. Serving the hardest user well was the bet.

Starting point & my role

The founder handed me a concept — called Azana at the time — and a semi-finished product document. A backend prototype existed, but there was no frontend or UI at all: the entire user-facing product was still to be defined and built. Over the first two weeks I ran research, set a brand identity, and moved into design; I also led the recruitment of the three founding engineers who built the foundation. From there I owned the product end-to-end through to its current pre-launch state — sequencing the 0→1 deliberately (research → brand → design system → delivery) with data trust as the through-line.

Four decisions

Each ladders back to the one-line problem — scattered and untrustworthy, no single view — and each is traceable to real work that shipped.

01
Framing

Make trust a visible product primitive

Context
Net worth is assembled from sources of very different reliability; one number hides how trustworthy each input is.
Decision
Made source, evidence, and freshness first-class, visible concepts on every value — with a recovery path for stale ones.
Why
The core failure was trust, not just fragmentation. Aggregating alone would just produce a prettier untrustworthy number.
Outcome
The product tells the truth about its own data; manual entry legitimized as a real valuation mode.
02
Focus

Separate personal & business by design

Context
The owner-operator's defining trait is a blurred personal/business line; naive net worth misleads either way.
Decision
Built a strict entity model (default Personal + Business entities), ownership as a Personal equity holding, and no blended cross-entity totals.
Why
Serve the hardest user precisely — while refusing to drift into consumer budgeting or SME-first accounting.
Outcome
Per-entity dashboards + an All-Entities overview without double-counting; cut the premature enterprise surface.
03
Design

Build the design language, not just screens

Context
As designer-PM with no existing frontend, "calm, premium, high-trust" had to become a real, consistent interface.
Decision
Designed and built a from-scratch design system (tokens → primitives → shell → pages) with trust-encoding primitives (source chips, calm sparklines).
Why
In fintech, trust is aesthetic as much as functional; a design system makes consistency itself a trust signal.
Outcome
A coherent system across the app, light and dark — the product feels as trustworthy as its data discipline.
04
Delivery

Ship the truth, not a prettier lie

Context
The hero net-worth trend chart was a stubbed, flat fake line — the exact failure the product exists to fix.
Decision
Refused to ship fabricated history; drove a real backend trend endpoint computed from actual historical sources.
Why
The problem is "untrustworthy" — a fake hero chart would undermine the whole thesis.
Outcome
A real, period-filterable trend with an honest empty state. The flagship chart tells the truth.

Trust, made visible

Craft

Outcome & status

Arzìqi is pre-launch and closed-beta-ready. V1 (ingestion, parsing, transactions, dashboards, anomalies, assets/liabilities) is shipped; V2 (the Personal Net Worth dashboard, source-backed records, market pricing, evidence documents, the entity model, and billing) is partially shipped. I authored 100+ merge requests across the rebrand, design system, entity model, onboarding, parser reliability, security, and the wealth dashboard.

The product hasn't launched, so there are no traction metrics to claim — the value here is the product judgment, the craft, and the launch-readiness.

Reflection

If I started again, I'd change the email-first entry. Leading new users into a mandatory email-connection pipeline assumed everyone's first move was to connect a mailbox; I later reframed onboarding to be asset/wealth-first, letting users add any account first. The lesson stuck because I didn't just note it — I shipped the correction.
